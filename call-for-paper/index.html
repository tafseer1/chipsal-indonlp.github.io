Call for Papers

The Joint Workshop on Challenges in Processing South Asian Languages (CHiPSAL) and Natural Language Processing for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages (IndoNLP) will be held on May 14–15, 2027, co-located with the 32nd International Conference on Computational Linguistics (COLING 2027) in Macau, China.

Overview

South Asia is home to hundreds of languages spoken by nearly two billion people across multiple major language families—most notably Indo-Aryan and Dravidian, alongside Tibeto-Burman, Austroasiatic, and others. Despite this immense linguistic diversity and rich cultural heritage, South Asian languages remain significantly under-resourced and under-represented in modern Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Model (LLM) research.

For COLING 2027, CHiPSAL and IndoNLP are joining forces to host a unified forum bringing together computational linguists, NLP researchers, speech technologists, and language-community practitioners. By uniting both communities, the joint workshop fosters cross-family comparative research, shared datasets and evaluation benchmarks, and collaborative digital infrastructure for South Asian language technologies.

Alignment with COLING 2027 Special Theme (“NLP for Linguistics”): In synergy with COLING 2027’s special theme, this joint workshop welcomes research that not only builds practical NLP tools for South Asian languages, but also leverages computational modeling to deepen scientific understanding of South Asian linguistic typology, dialectal variation, and underrepresented speech communities.

Focus of CHiPSAL and IndoNLP

  • CHiPSAL (Challenges in Processing South Asian Languages): Emphasizes foundational linguistic, script-level, and structural challenges across spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages. CHiPSAL addresses core technical bottlenecks including Unicode encoding and orthographic complexities, rich morphology, dialectal variation, standardization, pervasive code-mixing, digital heritage preservation, and systematic model benchmarking.
  • IndoNLP (Natural Language Processing for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages): Focuses on bridging the technological and resource gap specifically for Indo-Aryan and Dravidian Languages (IADL). IndoNLP highlights the creation and annotation of cleaned digital corpora, LLM adaptation for complex IADL syntax and semantics, speech and OCR technologies, cross-lingual systems, and downstream applications such as sentiment analysis, summarization, hate speech and offensive language detection, and combating misinformation, fake news, and spam.

Themes and Topics of Interest

We invite substantial, original, and unpublished research papers (long and short), resource/dataset papers, and system demonstrations covering spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages (with particular attention to Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, and regional South Asian varieties ). Topics include, but are not limited to:

1. Scripts, Orthography, and Core Linguistic Modeling

  • Encoding and Unicode issues in South Asian scripts
  • Orthographic complexities and their impact on language technology
  • Morphological analysis, generation, syntax, and semantic modeling in South Asian languages
  • Dialectal variations, language identification, dialect detection, and language standardisation
  • Code-mixing, code-switching, and multilingualism in South Asian contexts

2. Language Resources, Benchmarks, and Heritage Preservation

  • Developing cleaned Indo-Aryan and Dravidian language corpora (Unicode) and digital linguistic resources
  • Building annotated datasets for spoken, written, signed, and multimodal South Asian languages
  • Benchmarking and evaluating NLP models across South Asian languages and dialects
  • Preserving linguistic and cultural heritage through language technology

3. Large Language Models, Translation, and Speech/Vision Systems

  • Large Language Models (LLMs) for South Asian, Indo-Aryan, and Dravidian languages (pre-training, instruction tuning, alignment, and efficiency)
  • Machine translation and cross-lingual transfer systems
  • Speech technologies: Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Text-to-Speech (TTS) synthesis
  • Optical Character Recognition (OCR) systems, information extraction, and knowledge modelling

4. Downstream Applications and Online Safety

  • Sentiment analysis, opinion mining, and text summarisation
  • Hate speech, abusive language, and offensive content detection
  • Detection of fake news, rumors, spam, phishing, conspiracy theories, and misinformation
  • Real-world NLP applications in education, healthcare, governance, and accessibility for South Asian communities

Submission Guidelines (Based on COLING 2027)

Submission Tracks

  • Track 1 — Direct Workshop Submissions (Deadline: January 15, 2027): Intended for original, unpublished research submitted directly to the joint workshop. All direct submissions will undergo rigorous double-blind peer review by 3 reviewers with relevant domain expertise from the joint Program Committee.
  • Track 2 — COLING 2027 ARR Submissions (Deadline: February 15, 2027): Intended for papers previously submitted to the COLING 2027 ACL Rolling Review (ARR) cycle (following COLING 2027 main conference acceptance notifications on February 10, 2027). Authors must submit their manuscript accompanied by their ARR reviews, meta-review, and a brief response/revision summary explaining how reviewer feedback has been or will be addressed.

Formatting and Page Limits

  • Language & Templates: Submissions must be written in English and formatted using the official *ACL / COLING 2027 style templates (LaTeX, Overleaf, or Word).
  • Long Papers: Up to 8 pages of main content.
  • Short Papers & System Demonstrations: Up to 4 pages of main content.
  • Unlimited References and Appendices: Page limits apply strictly to the main body of the paper. Papers may include unlimited pages for references, technical appendices, data/code statements, and supplementary materials (such as source code, datasets, or multimedia appendices).
  • Camera-Ready Allowance: Upon acceptance, both long and short papers are granted 1 additional page of main content (up to 9 pages for long papers and 5 pages for short papers) to address reviewer comments.

Mandatory Sections, Anonymity, and Publication Policies

  • Mandatory Limitations Section: In accordance with COLING 2027 guidelines, all submissions must include a dedicated Limitations section at the end of the paper (after the Conclusion and before the References; excluded from the page limit).
  • Ethics and Data/Code Availability: Authors are strongly encouraged to include an Ethics Statement (excluded from the page limit) and a Data/Code Availability Statement. Newly created language resources are encouraged to be registered via open repositories or the LRE Map.
  • Double-Blind Anonymization: All direct submissions (including appendices and linked repositories) must be fully anonymized for double-blind review. Author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and self-identifying repository URLs must be removed from the initial submission.
  • Preprints and Concurrent Submissions: Consistent with COLING 2027 and ACL policies, there is no anonymity period restricting arXiv or preprint posting. Concurrent submissions to other venues must be declared at submission time; if accepted to the joint CHiPSAL–IndoNLP workshop, the paper must be withdrawn from all other archival venues.
  • Presentation and Proceedings: Accepted papers will be presented as either Oral or Poster / Poster+Demo presentations (with no distinction in the proceedings between oral and poster papers) and will be published in the official COLING 2027 Joint Workshop Proceedings in the ACL Anthology. At least one author of each accepted paper must register for and attend the workshop to present the work.
